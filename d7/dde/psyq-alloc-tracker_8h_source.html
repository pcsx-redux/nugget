27/*
28
29Ready-made allocator-tracker registrations for the Psy-Q allocators.
30
31Describing an allocator to Redux's tracker is a dozen lines of filling in
32function addresses and argument indices, and for the Sony libraries those lines
33are the same for everyone forever. They belong here rather than pasted into
34every program: an argument index typed wrong produces plausible garbage rather
35than an error, and a copy that drifts from its original is worse than no copy.
36
37Psy-Q ships THREE independent allocation systems (LibOver 2-23), so there are
38three functions here:
39
40 family 1 InitHeap malloc calloc realloc free "ROM-based"
41 family 2 InitHeap2 malloc2 calloc2 realloc2 free2 "RAM-based"
42 family 3 InitHeap3 malloc3 calloc3 realloc3 free3 "high-speed RAM-based"
43
44They have independent state and may be used simultaneously; registering more
45than one gives you more than one arena in the viewer. Family 1 is a set of
46trampolines into the kernel and carries a documented, unfixable bug - "the area
47allocated cannot be completely released in free()" - so a family-1 program
48leaks by design and the tracker will show exactly that. It is not a defect in
49the tracker and it is worth looking at once.
50
51Add src/mips/psyq/alloc-tracker/psyq-alloc-tracker.c to your SRCS and call one
52of these once, EARLY - ideally before your InitHeap, so the tracker sees the
53arena being created and takes its bounds from the call rather than needing them
54supplied. Registering later works, but everything allocated beforehand is
55invisible to it forever.
56
57All of this requires the interpreter with the debugger enabled. The tracker
58places execution breakpoints on the allocator's entry points, and the dynamic
59recompiler honors no execution breakpoints at all.
60
61*/
